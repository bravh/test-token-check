Part 1 of 3

Create a purchase order

The purchase order is your written promise to buy. Nothing else in this guide can happen until one exists and has been confirmed.

1

Open the list of purchase orders

From anywhere in Dynamics 365, open the navigation menu on the left and follow this path:

Modules › Procurement and sourcing › Purchase orders › All purchase orders

You will land on a list of every order your company has raised. This is your home base for Parts 1, 2 and 3 — you will come back here each time.

2

Tell the system who you are buying from

A small window opens on the right called Create purchase order. The first field, Vendor account, is the only one you have to fill in.

Click into it and start typing the vendor's name or account number. A list appears underneath and narrows as you type — click the right one. The vendor's full name fills in automatically next to it.

3

Open General and set where and when

Below the vendor there are collapsed sections. Click the word General to expand it, then check:

  • Site and Warehouse — where the goods will physically arrive. These usually fill in from the vendor, but they are worth a glance.
  • Delivery date — when you expect the goods. It is your best estimate, and you can change it later.
  • Currency — comes from the vendor record. Only change it if you know you should.

Then click OK at the bottom of the window.

4

List what you are buying

You are now on the order itself. The lower half of the page is called Purchase order lines — one line per thing you are buying.

Click Add line, then fill the row across:

  • Item number — start typing and pick from the list. The product name fills itself in.
  • Quantity — how many. Unit normally fills in on its own (pieces, boxes, kilos).
  • Unit price — if a price is already agreed with the vendor it appears by itself. If it is blank or wrong, type the correct one.
  • Site and Warehouse — carried down from the header. Change them only if this particular line goes somewhere else.

Press Tab to move across the row, or click straight into a cell. Repeat for each additional item.

5

Save

Click the Save icon at the top of the page, or press Ctrl + S. Until you save, the Confirm button in the next step will not work.

6

Confirm the order

Confirming is what turns a draft into a real commitment to the vendor. Go to the Purchase tab along the top of the page, and in the Actions group click Confirm.

The page reloads and the Document status at the top changes to Purchase order. That reads oddly on a page that is already called a purchase order, but it just means “a confirmed order document now exists” — and it is your signal that Part 1 is finished.

If Confirm is greyed out
  • You have unsaved changes. Save first.
  • Your company uses an approval workflow. In that case there is a Workflow button instead — click Submit, add a note, and wait for your approver. Confirm becomes available once the order is approved.
  • The order is already confirmed. Check the document status next to the order number.
  • You do not have permission to confirm orders. Ask whoever administers Dynamics 365 at your organisation.
Sending the order to the vendor

Confirming already produces the confirmation document, but it does not send it anywhere. Click Purchase order confirmation on the same Purchase tab to open the confirmations that have been generated, then print one or send it as a PDF from there. Many organisations automate this; check what yours does before sending anything twice.
