Buying something, receiving it,
and paying the bill

A step-by-step walkthrough of the purchase order, product receipt and vendor invoice flow. Written for people who use Dynamics 365 — not for people who build it.

5 parts About 25 minutes No technical knowledge needed
The big picture

Three documents, always in this order

Every purchase in Dynamics 365 creates three records, one after the other. You cannot skip a step, and each one unlocks the next. If you remember nothing else from this guide, remember this row:

Part 1

Purchase order

You promise to buy. You list what you want, from whom, at what price — then confirm it and send it to the vendor.

Status: Open order
Part 2

Product receipt

The goods turn up. You record what actually arrived, so the stock in the system matches the stock on the shelf.

Status: Received
Part 3

Vendor invoice

The bill arrives. You check it against the order and the receipt, then post it so Finance can pay the vendor.

Status: Invoiced
Why the order matters

Dynamics 365 compares all three documents before it lets an invoice be paid. This is called three-way matching: what you ordered, what you received, and what you were billed have to agree. Receiving before you order, or invoicing before you receive, breaks that chain — which is why the buttons for the later steps stay greyed out until the earlier one is done.

Before you start

Four things to have ready

  • You are in the right company. The company code sits in the top-right corner of the screen. Everything you create belongs to whichever company is showing there, and it cannot be moved afterwards.
  • The vendor already exists. You pick a vendor from a list — you do not type a new one on the fly. If yours is missing, ask whoever looks after vendor records to add it first.
  • You know your item numbers. Or the category to buy against, if you are buying a service rather than a stocked product.
  • You know your site and warehouse. These tell the system where the goods are going. Most people only ever use one of each — if you are not sure which, ask a colleague on your team.

The screens in this guide are simplified drawings, not photographs of a live system. Your own screen will carry your company's colours, extra fields and buttons your organisation has added, and menu items you do not have permission to use may be missing. The shape of the flow — and the order of the steps — is the same either way.
