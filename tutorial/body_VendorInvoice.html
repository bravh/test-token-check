Part 3 of 3

Record the vendor's invoice

The bill arrives. Your job is to check it against what was ordered and what was received, then post it so Finance can pay it. Dynamics 365 does most of the checking for you.

What three-way matching means in practice

The system lines up three numbers for every item: the price you agreed on the order, the quantity you recorded on the receipt, and the quantity and price on the vendor's invoice. Where all three agree, you get a green tick and can post. Where they do not, you get a warning — and the whole point of this step is to look at that warning before you pass the bill on.

1

Open the order and go to the Invoice tab

Open the purchase order as before, click the Invoice tab along the top, and in the Generate group click Invoice.

Modules › Procurement and sourcing › Purchase orders › All purchase orders › open the order › Invoice › Invoice

If invoices reach your company as scanned documents, they may already be waiting for you somewhere else instead:

Modules › Accounts payable › Invoices › Pending vendor invoices

Either route opens the same page, and everything from step 2 onwards is identical.

2

Copy the details off the vendor's bill

At the top of the invoice page, fill in three things from the paper or PDF in front of you:

  • Number — the vendor's own invoice number, exactly as they wrote it. This is not generated for you, and it is how duplicate bills get caught.
  • Invoice date — the date printed on the invoice, not today's date. Payment terms are calculated from it.
  • Invoice description — a short note in your own words, so the next person to open this knows what it was for.
3

Bring in the right receipts

Set Default quantity for lines to Product receipt quantity. That tells the system to bill you for what actually arrived rather than what was originally ordered — which is what you want in almost every case.

Then click Match product receipts and tick the delivery or deliveries this invoice covers. One invoice can cover several deliveries; equally, one delivery can be split across two invoices. This is where you say which is which.

4

Read the matching column

Each line now carries a small mark showing whether the numbers agree:

  • Green tick — quantity and price match the order and the receipt. Nothing to do.
  • Orange warning — something differs. Click Review to see exactly what: usually a price higher than agreed, or a quantity billed that was never received.

Never post through a warning you have not understood. If the vendor has genuinely charged more, that is a conversation with them, not a number to overwrite.

5

Check the total, then post

Compare the total at the bottom of the page with the total on the vendor's invoice, including tax. If they differ by a few cents it is usually rounding; if they differ by more, find out why first.

When you are happy, click Post.

6

What you have just done

  • The order's status becomes Invoiced. For most orders that is the end of its life.
  • An open amount appears on the vendor's account, ready for the next payment run. Finance takes it from here — nothing is paid from this screen.
  • A permanent record sits under Invoice › Journals › Invoice, tying the bill back to the receipt and the order.
When Post will not let you through
  • A matching difference is above the tolerance your company allows. The invoice can be saved but not posted until somebody with authority approves the difference. Save it, and pass it to your manager or to Accounts payable.
  • “Invoice number already used for this vendor.” Almost always a duplicate bill. Check the vendor's account before entering it again.
  • The invoice date falls in a closed accounting period. Finance closes months to stop late entries. Ask them whether to reopen the period or to use a later date.
  • Nothing has been received yet. An invoice with no receipt behind it has nothing to match against. Go back and finish Part 2.
