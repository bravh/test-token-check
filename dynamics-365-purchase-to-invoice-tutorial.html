Buying something, receiving it,
and paying the bill

A step-by-step walkthrough of the purchase order, product receipt and vendor invoice flow. Written for people who use Dynamics 365 — not for people who build it.

3 steps 5 pages About 25 minutes No technical knowledge needed
The big picture

Three documents, always in this order

Every purchase in Dynamics 365 creates three records, one after the other. You cannot skip a step, and each one unlocks the next. If you remember nothing else from this guide, remember this sequence:

Part 1

Purchase order

You promise to buy. You list what you want, from whom, at what price — then confirm it and send it to the vendor.

Status: Open order
Part 2

Product receipt

The goods turn up. You record what actually arrived, so the stock in the system matches the stock on the shelf.

Status: Received
Part 3

Vendor invoice

The bill arrives. You check it against the order and the receipt, then post it so Finance can pay the vendor.

Status: Invoiced
Why the order matters

Dynamics 365 compares all three documents before it lets an invoice be posted. This is called three-way matching: what you ordered, what you received, and what you were billed have to agree. Each document is built from the one before it, so working out of order gets you nowhere — an invoice raised against nothing received has no quantities to check itself against.

Before you start

Four things to have ready

  • You are in the right company. Dynamics 365 calls this the legal entity, and it is shown in the top corner of the screen. Everything you create belongs to whichever company is showing there, and it cannot be moved afterwards.
  • The vendor already exists. You pick a vendor from a list — you do not type a new one on the fly. If yours is missing, ask whoever looks after vendor records to add it first.
  • You know your item numbers. If you are buying a service rather than a physical product you pick a procurement category instead of an item number — ask your team which one to use.
  • You know your site and warehouse. These tell the system where the goods are going. Most people only ever use one of each — if you are not sure which, ask a colleague on your team.

Part 1 of 3

Create a purchase order

The purchase order is your written promise to buy. Nothing else in this guide can happen until one exists and has been confirmed.

1

Open the list of purchase orders

From anywhere in Dynamics 365, open the navigation menu on the left and follow this path:

Modules › Procurement and sourcing › Purchase orders › All purchase orders

You will land on a list of every order your company has raised. This is your home base for Parts 1, 2 and 3 — you will come back here each time.

2

Tell the system who you are buying from

A small window opens on the right called Create purchase order. The first field, Vendor account, is the only one you have to fill in.

Click into it and start typing the vendor's name or account number. A list appears underneath and narrows as you type — click the right one. The vendor's full name fills in automatically next to it.

3

Open General and set where and when

Below the vendor there are collapsed sections. Click the word General to expand it, then check:

  • Site and Warehouse — where the goods will physically arrive. These usually fill in from the vendor, but they are worth a glance.
  • Delivery date — when you expect the goods. It is your best estimate, and you can change it later.
  • Currency — comes from the vendor record. Only change it if you know you should.

Then click OK at the bottom of the window.

4

List what you are buying

You are now on the order itself. The lower half of the page is called Purchase order lines — one line per thing you are buying.

Click Add line, then fill the row across:

  • Item number — start typing and pick from the list. The product name fills itself in.
  • Quantity — how many. Unit normally fills in on its own (pieces, boxes, kilos).
  • Unit price — if a price is already agreed with the vendor it appears by itself. If it is blank or wrong, type the correct one.
  • Site and Warehouse — carried down from the header. Change them only if this particular line goes somewhere else.

Press Tab to move across the row, or click straight into a cell. Repeat for each additional item.

5

Save

Click the Save icon at the top of the page, or press Ctrl + S. Until you save, the Confirm button in the next step will not work.

6

Confirm the order

Confirming is what turns a draft into a real commitment to the vendor. Go to the Purchase tab along the top of the page, and in the Actions group click Confirm.

The page reloads and the Document status at the top changes to Purchase order. That reads oddly on a page that is already called a purchase order, but it just means “a confirmed order document now exists” — and it is your signal that Part 1 is finished.

If Confirm is greyed out
  • You have unsaved changes. Save first.
  • Your company uses an approval workflow. In that case there is a Workflow button instead — click Submit, add a note, and wait for your approver. Confirm becomes available once the order is approved.
  • The order is already confirmed. Check the document status next to the order number.
  • You do not have permission to confirm orders. Ask whoever administers Dynamics 365 at your organisation.
Sending the order to the vendor

Confirming already produces the confirmation document, but it does not send it anywhere. Click Purchase order confirmation on the same Purchase tab to open the confirmations that have been generated, then print one or send it as a PDF from there. Many organisations automate this; check what yours does before sending anything twice.

Part 2 of 3

Receive the goods

When the delivery arrives at your door, you record it. This is called posting a product receipt, and it is what makes the stock in Dynamics 365 match the stock on your shelf.

Have the vendor's paperwork in front of you

Every delivery comes with a packing slip or delivery note, and it carries a reference number. You will be asked to type that number in. It is how anyone looking at this order later can tie the entry in Dynamics 365 back to a piece of paper in a folder.

1

Open the confirmed order

Go back to the list of orders and click the order number to open it.

Modules › Procurement and sourcing › Purchase orders › All purchase orders

You are looking for one with the status Open order that has already been confirmed. If it is easier, filter the list by vendor.

2

Go to the Receive tab

Along the top of the order there is a row of tabs. Click Receive. The buttons underneath change. In the Generate group, click Product receipt.

3

Type the vendor's reference and the date

Two fields matter here:

  • Product receipt — copy the number from the vendor's packing slip. It is required, and it must be different from any receipt number already used on this order. If the delivery note carries no number at all, use something your team agrees on and sticks to — the arrival date plus the vendor name, for instance.
  • Product receipt date — the day the goods actually arrived, which is not always today. Get this right: it decides the accounting period the stock lands in.
4

Say how much actually turned up

The Quantity box near the top decides what the lines are filled with:

  • Ordered quantity — use this when the whole delivery arrived complete. Every line is filled with the full amount.
  • Deliver now — use this when only part of it arrived. The lines are filled with whatever you type in the Deliver now column, and you type the real figures yourself.

Scroll to the Lines grid at the bottom of the window and check every row against the boxes in front of you. Two short of what was ordered? Type the number you actually got. Nothing at all on a line? Type 0 and that line stays open for a later delivery.

5

Post it

Click OK. The window closes and the order reloads. If you need a printed copy for the warehouse, tick Print product receipt before you click.

6

Check that it worked

Three things change straight away, and they are all worth a glance:

  • The order's Status becomes Received if everything came, or stays Open order with a partial amount showing if some is still outstanding.
  • The Received column on the lines now shows the amounts you entered, and Remaining drops accordingly.
  • Anything still outstanding stays on the order. When the rest of the delivery arrives, come back and post a second product receipt with the new packing slip number — you can do this as many times as the delivery is split.
  • A record of the receipt appears under Receive › Journals › Product receipt. Open it if you ever need to prove what was received, when, and against which packing slip.
The three that trip people up
  • “Product receipt number already exists.” That reference has been used on this order before. Check whether somebody already posted this delivery before you type a new number.
  • You cannot receive more than you ordered unless your company allows over-delivery. If more turned up than the order says, speak to whoever raised the order rather than forcing the number through.
  • Posting cannot be undone by deleting it. If you get a quantity wrong, the fix is to post a correcting receipt with a negative amount — ask a colleague to walk you through it the first time.
If your warehouse registers arrivals first

Some organisations scan goods in at the loading bay before anyone posts a receipt — through Arrival overview or a handheld scanner. If that is how yours works, the quantities are already recorded by the time you get here: choose Registered quantity in the Quantity box instead of typing amounts yourself, and the rest of the step is identical.

Part 3 of 3

Record the vendor's invoice

The bill arrives. Your job is to check it against what was ordered and what was received, then post it so Finance can pay it. Dynamics 365 does most of the checking for you.

What three-way matching means in practice

The system lines up three numbers for every item: the price you agreed on the order, the quantity you recorded on the receipt, and the quantity and price on the vendor's invoice. Where all three agree, you get a green tick and can post. Where they do not, you get a warning — and the whole point of this step is to look at that warning before you pass the bill on.

1

Open the order and go to the Invoice tab

Open the purchase order as before, click the Invoice tab along the top, and in the Generate group click Invoice.

Modules › Procurement and sourcing › Purchase orders › All purchase orders › open the order › Invoice › Invoice

If invoices reach your company as scanned documents, they may already be waiting for you somewhere else instead:

Modules › Accounts payable › Invoices › Pending vendor invoices

Either route opens the same page, and everything from step 2 onwards is identical.

2

Copy the details off the vendor's bill

At the top of the invoice page, fill in three things from the paper or PDF in front of you:

  • Number — the vendor's own invoice number, exactly as they wrote it. This is not generated for you, and it is how duplicate bills get caught.
  • Invoice date — the date printed on the invoice, not today's date. Payment terms are calculated from it.
  • Invoice description — a short note in your own words, so the next person to open this knows what it was for.
3

Bring in the right receipts

Set Default quantity for lines to Product receipt quantity. That tells the system to bill you for what actually arrived rather than what was originally ordered — which is what you want in almost every case.

Then click Match product receipts and tick the delivery or deliveries this invoice covers. One invoice can cover several deliveries; equally, one delivery can be split across two invoices. This is where you say which is which.

The quantities now fill in from those receipts — 40 rolls, in our example, because that is all that arrived. If the vendor has billed a different amount, type what they billed into the Invoiced column. That is what puts the difference on screen instead of quietly paying the wrong figure.

4

Read the matching column

Each line now carries a small mark showing whether the numbers agree:

  • Green tick — quantity and price match the order and the receipt. Nothing to do.
  • Orange warning — something differs. Click Matching details on the Review tab to see exactly what: usually a price higher than agreed, or a quantity billed that was never received.

Never post through a warning you have not understood. If the vendor has genuinely charged more, that is a conversation with them, not a number to overwrite.

In our example there are two honest ways forward: put the invoiced quantity back to 40 and post, which pays for what arrived and leaves the other 20 rolls open on the order for a later bill; or leave the invoice unposted and ask the vendor for a corrected one. Which of those you take is your company’s call, not the system’s.

5

Check the total, then post

The order total you saw in Part 1 was before tax; the invoice adds tax on top, so the figure here is normally higher than the order total. That on its own is not a problem.

Compare the total at the bottom of the page with the total on the vendor's invoice, including tax. If they differ by a few cents it is usually rounding; if they differ by more, find out why first.

When you are happy, click Post.

6

What you have just done

  • Once everything on the order has been received and billed, its status becomes Invoiced and the order is finished. While something is still outstanding — our 20 rolls — it stays Open order.
  • An open amount appears on the vendor's account, ready for the next payment run. Finance takes it from here — nothing is paid from this screen.
  • A permanent record sits under Invoice › Journals › Invoice, tying the bill back to the receipt and the order.
When Post will not let you through
  • A matching difference is above the tolerance your company allows. The invoice can be saved but not posted until somebody with authority approves the difference. Save it, and pass it to your manager or to Accounts payable.
  • “Invoice number already used for this vendor.” Almost always a duplicate bill. Check the vendor's account before entering it again.
  • The invoice date falls in a closed accounting period. Finance closes months to stop late entries. Ask them whether to reopen the period or to use a later date.
  • Nothing has been received yet. An invoice with no receipt behind it has nothing to match against. Go back and finish Part 2.

When it will not work

The problems people actually hit

Confirm is greyed out

Save the order first. If it is still greyed out, your company requires approval — use Workflow › Submit and wait, or you may simply not have permission to confirm.

The product receipt number is rejected

It has been used on this order before. Check whether the delivery was already posted by someone else before you invent a new number.

You received more than was ordered

Most companies block this. Talk to whoever raised the order about increasing the quantity rather than forcing the receipt through.

The invoice will not post

Look at the Match column. A difference in price or quantity beyond your company's tolerance needs approval, not a correction typed over the top. Save the invoice and pass it on.

You posted something wrong

Posted documents are not deleted — they are corrected with a reversing entry. Do not try to fix it by editing or deleting. Ask Accounts payable or a colleague who has done it before.

Plain English

Words you will keep seeing

PostTo commit a document for real. Posting updates stock and accounts, and cannot be undone by deleting.
Product receiptThe record of goods arriving. Also the name of the vendor's packing slip number you type in.
Deliver nowThe quantity arriving in this particular delivery, as opposed to the quantity ordered.
Three-way matchingThe automatic check that the order, the receipt and the invoice agree with one another.
Site and WarehouseWhere goods physically live. Site is the location; warehouse is a storage area within it.
Legal entityThe company you are working in, shown in the top corner. Records cannot be moved between companies.
Accounts payableThe part of the system, and the team, that pays vendors.
